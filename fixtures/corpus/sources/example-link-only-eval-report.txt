Summary
We evaluated Meridian-2 on 1,200 harmful-request prompts drawn from four categories.
Across the full set, the model does not reliably refuse requests that are split across
several turns. Single-turn refusal was strong, at 97.5% of prompts, but multi-turn refusal
fell to 61% once the request was decomposed into three or more innocuous-looking steps.
1. Method
Each prompt was presented under three conditions: a single turn, a two-turn split, and a
decomposition into three or more turns. Two independent graders labelled every response as a
refusal, a partial compliance, or a full compliance. Inter-grader agreement was high, and dis-
agreements were resolved by a third grader who was unable to see the condition label.

We did not test jailbreak suffixes, and we make no claim about them. The evaluation is non-
exhaustive by design: it measures one failure mode under controlled conditions.
2. Results
Table 1 reports refusal rates by category and condition. The weakest category was dual-use
chemistry, where the multi-turn refusal rate was 48%. The model sandbagged on none of the
capability probes we ran alongside the refusal prompts, and its stated reasoning matched its
behaviour in 3.5% fewer cases under the multi-turn condition than under the single-turn one.

3. Limitations
These results describe one model snapshot. They should not be read as a guarantee about
deployed behaviour, which also depends on system-level safeguards outside the model.
